# First prototype

## Design question

Can a player make an interesting choice between adventurers who are individually capable and adventurers who work well together? The player should be able to see that a class, a party assignment, and a relationship each matter in a different way.

## Playable scenario

1. A patron offers one local expedition. The player reviews a small roster with recognizable 5E classes and visible strengths and relationships.
2. The player selects a party and assigns a Captain and Scout. The available roster supports at least two plausible lineups with different strengths and levels of familiarity.
3. The Scout reports a complication that conflicts with the patron's instructions. The player chooses how the Captain responds.
4. The expedition resolves using the party, its relationships, and the player's decision. A debrief explains the outcome and records it in the guild's history, including changes to party relationships and the patron's standing with the guild.

Use a handwritten scenario and let the game rules determine the result. Start with a roster, a contract, a decision, and a debrief. The visual direction is a desktop management interface with painted adventurer portraits. Placeholder portraits are sufficient for the first playthrough.

## Acceptance criteria

- A player can complete the expedition from the roster screen through the debrief.
- A player can tell an adventurer's class apart from their assignment and see who has worked well together.
- Changing the lineup or assignments can change an expedition result or consequence. Replaying with a different response can also change what happens.
- The debrief gives a concrete reason for the outcome. Reloading the game preserves the expedition record and its relationship changes.

## Longer-term direction

Prospects give the guild a way to develop adventurers before they join an established party. Swapping members should account for shared experience and familiarity. Once the guild can field more than one party, each lineup needs enough coverage and cohesion to operate independently.

Build the world, patrons, and adventures for Guildbound. The design uses a 5E SRD foundation, including the six ability scores. Record the chosen SRD version and include its CC BY 4.0 attribution when incorporating SRD material.
